# Vanilla art reference atlas — working research notes

Under [Visual QA](VISUAL-QA.md) and [research evidence](VANILLA-RESEARCH.md). Official Minecraft Java 1.21.1, client SHA-1 30c73b1c5da787909b2f73340419fdf13b9def88. Original R9 catalog: 57 directly inspected source PNGs, all 57 official-client hashes confirmed by [research CI](https://github.com/MngkLrd/gradlejar/actions/runs/46029695582), and 28 executable material/role rules. R11 adds 43 reference IDs and 16 role-specific rules for items and equipment; official-client verification of the extension is part of research CI. Its use is mandatory in applicable texture review; the historical initial sample described below is not the current coverage. This is a practical interpretation of observed art, not official Mojang instructions, a universal drawing algorithm or an automatic review.

Use texture IDs relative to assets/minecraft/textures. Open actual verified PNGs at 1x and nearest-neighbor enlargement before drawing. Use the existing art_compare tool, official local client or version-pinned mirror verified against the official client. Do not redistribute vanilla PNGs. For tint/overlay art inspect the actual rendered reference when necessary; base grayscale and in-game color are different evidence.

| Material/role | Inspected reference IDs | Construction recipe to try | Counterexample to simplistic QA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vertical bark | block/oak_log, block/spruce_log, block/dark_oak_log, block/acacia_log | Start with unequal-width vertical ridges, break them with short offsets, use neighboring shades for turning planes, preserve a tileable rhythm, adjust material palette against the actual species | These four share identical color partitions; quality cannot require distinct layout per species |
| Horizontal bark | block/birch_log, block/jungle_log | Establish broad pale birch planes with local dark lenticels, or jungle layered horizontal streaks; avoid adding vertical stripes merely because the object is wood | Direction of clusters is material-specific, not always vertical |
| Log ends | block/oak_log_top, block/spruce_log_top | Establish bark boundary and broad rings; break exact contours locally with close shades and offsets; preserve heartwood/sapwood readability | Rings can be substantially shared across species; don't create arbitrary extra rings |
| Needles | block/spruce_leaves | Place small directed needle groups along twig-like motifs; cut out gaps following structure; limit value changes so tinted foliage reads as a mass | 133 four-connected components for 160 visible pixels, largest area 3; small components can be intentional |
| Broadleaf | block/oak_leaves, block/birch_leaves, block/acacia_leaves | Create uneven neighboring leaf groups, varying openings and local value relationships; retain leaf shapes across the tiled surface; inspect tint separately | Oak and birch each use four visible RGB colors; increasing colors is not required |
| Soil/stone | block/dirt, block/stone | Establish varied host aggregates, short irregular connected boundaries and restrained local contrasts; add embedded gray inclusions only if appropriate to material | Stone uses four colors; large coherent connected regions are not automatically flat |
| Ore | block/iron_ore | Keep rock as material background; vary shape of inclusions; lighter mineral interior and purposeful darker adjoining pixels describe embedded fragments | Count of colors is secondary to whether inclusions read as part of the rock |
| Armor/clothing | item/leather_chestplate | Establish collar, sleeves, waist and outline; place folds with adjacent shades; shade the outline to support the form; inspect tint and actual overlay/equipped context | Grayscale base is not the final leather palette; don't mistake tint for missing hue |
| Saplings | block/spruce_sapling, block/jungle_sapling, block/birch_sapling | Establish species structure before leaf details: spruce taper and layers, jungle irregular narrow stem and side branches, birch light asymmetrical leafy crown; attach foliage to purposeful stems | A spruce is not a birch with another palette; visible silhouette and placement of foliage differ | 
| Glass vessel | item/glass_bottle | Narrow neck, stepped shoulder, round lower body; use intentional empty interior and a few bright cool edge highlights to convey transparent walls; cork has its own small warm palette | Only 42 visible pixels, but 8 colors and clear object shape; empty space is meaningful, not missing detail |
| Metal vessel | item/bucket | Dark inner opening and rim, strong narrow highlights on the illuminated side, turning side plane, dark base; check that rim reads as a cavity | 8 colors; large local regions can coexist with sharp highlights and contour shading |
| Copper/iron | item/copper_ingot, item/iron_ingot | Establish upper plane and side plane, then local highlights/edge transitions; copper uses more interleaved shades, iron broad light planes and bright rim | Both have 8 colors and 135 visible pixels, but 63 vs 51 connected components; identical counts do not imply identical surface appearance |
| Clay/brick | item/brick | Clearly separate top and side using warm matte values, local edge accents and quieter broad faces than polished metal | 7 colors and 135 visible pixels; material can read without specular white streaks or excessive texture noise |
| Leather | item/leather | Start with recognizable stretched hide outline and folded corners; use dark outline, broad warm inner shapes, subordinate marks along bends | 6 colors, 128 visible pixels; a meaningful shape does not need a large palette |
| Food | item/bread, item/apple | Bread: oblique loaf, darker crust and local scoring, ochre/brown ramps. Apple: round form, stem, concentrated red/pink highlights and darker turning contours | Different food surfaces need different highlight placement even at same icon size |
| Oxidized copper | block/oxidized_copper | Treat oxidation as part of the surface: related teal/green clusters, lighter rim and darker boundaries, maintain coherent tile rhythm; inspect earlier oxidation states before designing a progression | This fully oxidized block is a color/material reference, not evidence for the placement of a small patch of patina on a flask |

Reference scope of the initial table: these are observations of 28 images, not every asset in the corpus. At that initial stage, tools, GUI, particles, entity UVs, animation and additional materials still needed direct visual study; R9 subsequently added tool examples, but GUI, particles, entity UVs and animation still need broader artistic coverage; R3 perturbation source selection does not imply visual inspection. Do not invent acceptance thresholds from summary statistics. If a candidate family is not covered, select and inspect actual comparable vanilla references using Visual QA.

## R9 additional material observations and mandatory rule index

24 further PNGs opened at native size and 8x, block surfaces also inspected as 3x3 tiles; all exact bytes checked against the official corpus SHA-256 in R7 artifact 15212406511. Together with the previous 33, the R9 direct sample reached 57. The canonical executable rule IDs, supported material profiles, exact references and limitations are in `tools/qa/research/vanilla-art-rules.json`. Apply its actual applicable row during drawing and review as required by [Visual QA](VISUAL-QA.md).

| Added family | Actual viewed examples | Observations and exceptions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Planks | block/birch_planks, block/spruce_planks | Horizontal board joints, small overlapping grain clusters inside each board. Compare oak; the same structural pattern can be legitimate across different species. Repetition describes constructed boards, unlike mechanically striped soil. |
| Masonry and clay | block/bricks, block/terracotta | Brick edges and mortar separate discrete objects, terracotta has intentionally subtle, nearly flat matte variations. Do not demand dramatic contrast for every material. |
| Textile | block/white_wool, block/red_wool | Dense close neighboring shades create soft fibrous texture, 40 and 36 visible RGB colors respectively. Large palettes can be purposeful; do not imitate this density on smooth metal. |
| Glass, ice, snow | block/glass, block/ice, block/snow | Glass uses transparent interior with edge/corner reflections, ice has all 256 pixels partially transparent with blue diagonal streaks, snow has only three near-white shades. Alpha and palette count depend on material and renderer. |
| Granular and rock | block/sand, block/gravel, block/cobblestone, block/deepslate, block/moss_block | Sand small low-contrast granules; gravel angular irregular fragments; cobble larger stony faces; deepslate layered dark bands; moss dense green growth. Each surface needs its own scale, cluster arrangement and material contrast. |
| Metal | block/copper_block, block/exposed_copper, block/weathered_copper, block/iron_block, block/gold_block | Copper has diagonal glints and changes from copper-colored to green regions with exposure; iron has horizontal soft streaks and a clear rim; gold high contrast diagonal reflections. Constructed metal seams/borders may repeat. Patina and light are surface structure, not unrelated accent spots. |
| Tools | item/iron_sword, item/wooden_pickaxe, item/diamond_pickaxe, item/shears | Working edge/head and handle use separate ramps, narrow local highlights, dark contour and functional outline. Pickaxe progression can reuse geometry; material changes must be readable. Shears' two blades and handles distinguish the function. |
| Paper | item/paper | Quiet broad white face and stepped gray edge; adding arbitrary detail to avoid flatness would reduce readability. |

No automatic aesthetic threshold follows from any row. 57 is a deliberately expanded exploratory sample, not a random population estimate. GUI, entity UVs, particles, animated assets and actual renderer behavior still lack sufficient direct visual evidence. Use the custom rule with an actual comparator for an uncovered category, not forced analogy to these static textures.

## Handoff for the independent reviewer

Use `tools.qa.vanilla_benchmark` privately with a fresh seed; the existing 40-pair pilot and expanded 240-pair suite are fixed exploratory controls, not a clean future holdout (glass_bottle has since been directly studied). The expanded set has 60 distinct RGB-layout fingerprints with zero exact overlap against the pilot and atlas, not guaranteed perceptual or family independence. Reserve fresh source families/layout groups for a definitive test; give a fresh reviewer only the rendered A/B sheets and instructions, no source key, filenames, metrics, author findings or seed. Ask for A/B/UNSURE source choice and separate specific material/shape/cluster/light observations and aesthetic accept/reject/unsure for each image. Freeze responses before revealing the key. Source identification accuracy is not aesthetic accuracy. For real product judgments, add owner-rejected and owner-approved assets with exact hashes; include difficult good-faith art, not just obvious synthetic shuffles. Report false acceptance and false rejection only against actual labeled acceptance cases. Current independent reviewer result: NOT_RUN.

## Real mod comparison: production lessons

See [R8 observed real assets](VANILLA-REAL-ART-REVIEW.md). For ground textures, view 3x3 at native scale and enlarged before choosing a variant; look for recurring diagonal/horizontal bands and identical high-contrast inclusion positions. The five pairs show how breaking mechanical bands into irregular material-specific aggregates helps; a relative improvement does not certify the tile. For groups of containers, compare the whole family in one native-scale sheet: clay, copper and leather must differ in contour/construction and material light response, not simply hue or a different accent pixel. A clear outline alone is not a finished icon. Review actual intended function before adding seams, rims, holes or fittings. These are exploratory observations, not numerical gates.
